## RULE 1 — REMOVE ALL TIMESTAMPS FIRST

Before translating, completely remove all timestamps, transcription timing markers, and video-duration labels.

Examples:

- `1:251 minute, 25 seconds`
- `2:032 minutes, 3 seconds`
- `10:0410 minutes, 4 seconds`
- `25:0625 minutes, 6 seconds`
- `[00:12:45]`
- `(12:45)`
- `00:15`
- `1 hour, 23 minutes`

These are transcription artifacts, NOT part of the spoken narration.

Remove them without deleting or modifying the actual story words surrounding them.

**Important:** Preserve times mentioned as part of the actual story, such as "رات دو بجے"، "صبح پانچ بج کر دس منٹ" or a date relevant to the investigation.

Although the writing appears in Hindi script, the spoken language is often actually Urdu or Hindustani.

## RULE 3 — CORRECT ALL HINDI SPEECH-TO-TEXT ERRORS

The input may contain incorrectly recognized, broken, combined, or distorted words.

You must detect and correct these errors throughout the ENTIRE story.

Examples of typical problems:

- Incorrect letters
- Missing letters
- Broken words
- Two words mistakenly joined together
- One word mistakenly divided into multiple words
- Incorrect Urdu vocabulary represented phonetically in Hindi
- Similar-sounding but incorrect words
- Incorrect grammatical endings
- Inconsistent person and place names
- Wrong gender agreement
- Incorrect dialogue punctuation

Use sentence meaning and context to determine the most likely intended expression.

For example:

Incorrect:  
"वो जा वकूआ का मुयना करने गया"

Correct:  
"وہ جائے وقوعہ کا معائنہ کرنے گیا۔"

Incorrect:  
"मैंने मकतला के खाविंद से सवालात किए"

Correct:  
"میں نے مقتولہ کے خاوند سے سوالات کیے۔"

Incorrect:  
"उस शख्स की बात मेरी समझ में नहीं आई"

Correct:  
"اس شخص کی بات میری سمجھ میں نہیں آئی۔"

Never preserve an obvious transcription error simply because it appears in the source.

However, if a person's name, date, number, place, or crucial evidence is genuinely ambiguous, **do not invent a correction**. Preserve the most defensible reading and record the uncertainty separately.

## RULE 4 — CONVERT INTO NATURAL, PROFESSIONAL PAKISTANI URDU

The finished Urdu must be:

- Grammatically correct
- Orthographically accurate
- Naturally flowing
- Easy to understand when narrated aloud
- Suitable for YouTube storytelling
- Faithful to the original speaker's voice
- Consistent with historical police investigation stories

Avoid mechanical word-for-word transliteration.

Avoid awkward Hindi sentence constructions.

Avoid unnecessary modern English vocabulary when a natural Urdu equivalent is available.

Use appropriate Urdu expressions such as:

- حسبِ معمول
- اطلاع موصول ہوئی
- جائے وقوعہ
- تفتیش
- مقتول
- مقتولہ
- آلۂ قتل
- مشکوک حالات
- سوال و جواب
- ابتدائی رپورٹ
- چشم دید گواہ
- سراغ
- شواہد

- پوسٹ مارٹم رپورٹ
- قتل کی واردات
- زیرِ حراست
- تفتیشی کارروائی

Do not make the Urdu unnecessarily difficult, overly poetic, or artificially formal.

The narration should feel like an experienced police officer naturally recounting an old investigation.

## RULE 5 — PRESERVE THE COMPLETE ORIGINAL STORY

This is one of the most important rules.

You MUST preserve:

1. All original events
2. Every meaningful dialogue exchange
3. The original investigation sequence
4. Character relationships
5. Original names and locations
6. Dates and timings
7. Evidence and forensic details
8. Suspicious circumstances
9. Police procedures
10. Plot twists and revelations
11. The identity of the murderer
12. The original ending and conclusion

**Do NOT:**

- Summarize the story
- Shorten the narration
- Remove important passages
- Add new scenes
- Invent clues
- Introduce new characters
- Change the murderer
- Alter the motive
- Rewrite the investigation
- Add dramatic hooks
- Create a different ending
- Add fictional police procedures or scientific evidence

If the original story contains repetition that is meaningful to the narrator's reasoning, retain it.

If the source contains an apparent factual contradiction, preserve it unless there is clear transcription evidence of the intended correction. Do not silently rewrite the plot to make it more logical.

## RULE 6 — MAINTAIN THE ORIGINAL STORYTELLING STYLE

The source stories are usually narrated in first person by a police investigating officer.

Maintain that perspective.

For example:

"میں حسبِ معمول صبح تیار ہو کر تھانے پہنچا تو حوالدار نے مجھے ایک قتل کی اطلاع دی۔"

Preserve the narrator's investigative thinking, observations, hesitation, doubt, and gradual understanding.

Maintain natural exchanges between the investigating officer, complainants, suspects, witnesses, constables, medical officers, and other characters.

Do not turn the narration into a modern screenplay.

Do not insert narrator labels, scene headings, sound effects, or video production notes.

Verify the order of clues, evidence, suspect statements, discoveries, and final revelation.

### PASS 8 — Voiceover readiness

Read through the complete narration as natural spoken Urdu, correcting awkward expressions without changing the plot.

### PASS 9 — Final comparison

Compare the final Urdu script with the entire original source one last time.

Never claim that all checks passed unless they were actually performed.
